# Research

## Summary

Investigate a question and produce an evidence-backed synthesis with visible uncertainty.

## Recommended

- Turn a broad topic into a concrete research question whose answer could change a decision.
- State the scope, freshness requirement, and source-quality requirement before collecting evidence.
- Prefer primary or first-party sources for factual claims, and record the source and date for each material finding.
- Distinguish sourced facts, interpretation, and inference; preserve relevant competing explanations or conflicting evidence.
- Explain what the evidence does not establish, including context limits, uncertainty, and unresolved gaps.
- End with a concise synthesis and a deliverable that maps conclusions back to the evidence collected.

## Avoid

- Filling evidence gaps silently or presenting an inference as a sourced fact.
- Treating a search result, benchmark, anecdote, or single source as universally decisive without checking scope and method.
- Ignoring source freshness when the question concerns changing products, policies, prices, or capabilities.
- Collecting citations without explaining how they support the conclusion.
